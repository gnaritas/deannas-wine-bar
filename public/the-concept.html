The concept

Two counters, one room

Deanna’s Wine Bar and Bert’s Pig Shop share one storefront, one kitchen and one idea: the pig arrives whole, everything gets used, and the wine is chosen to drink with it.

One farm, start to finish

The pork program is not a supplier arrangement — it is a family farm. Bert’s Pig Shop works hogs from Peculiar Pig, a fifth-generation farm on ancestral land near the Edisto River in upper Dorchester County, where the herd grazes and forages across wooded lots and the diet is finished on heritage grains. Whole hogs come in; fresh cuts, sausages and charcuterie go out to the counter the same week.

Licensed to do it on the premises

Making charcuterie where you serve it takes a South Carolina Department of Agriculture license — and a hazard plan thick enough to stand up on its own. We hold one. That license is what lets the case in front of you come out of the same building the hogs did.

The wine, priced like a neighborhood should drink it

Sommelier Andres Contreras built the list from tastings and a deep dive through wine archives: small European and domestic makers, reviving bottlings that fell off the shelves, poured at prices that invite a second glass. Boards and sandwiches are built to match it.

When do you open?
By the end of 2026. The exact day, the hours and the full list of boards go up on this site first — and people on the opening-day list get the email before anyone else.
Is it a restaurant, a bar, or a butcher shop?
All three, in one room. Deanna’s Wine Bar is the bar and dining room; Bert’s Pig Shop is the counter — one kitchen, one whole-hog program, one storefront on Wappoo Road.
Where does the meat come from?
Peculiar Pig, a fifth-generation family farm on 12 acres of ancestral land in upper Dorchester County. The hogs graze and forage on wooded lots and get supplemented with heritage grains.
Do you have a menu?
Sandwiches, charcuterie boards and wine from day one. Prices and the full list post here the same week we open.
